Edmonton Transit Service is committed to providing safe, accessible, reliable transportation to all of our riders, with barrier-free services, programs and features that make public transit easy and convenient.
Transit centres, LRT stations, and bus shelters are designed with barrier-free access in mind. Features include automatic doors, curb cuts and pararamps, bench seating with armrests, unobstructed sight lines, security lighting, and mobility aid access.
TTY Phones
As of January 1, 2023, the City directs TTY service to TELUS TTY (711). This service assists the deaf and hard of hearing over the phone.
Dial 711 from your TTY device
The TELUS Relay agent will answer by typing "TELUS RELAY SERVICE, THIS IS (agent's name & number)", followed by GA (for "go ahead")
Type 780-442-5311, then type GA
The TELUS Relay agent will dial the number and will let you know how the connection is progressing for example (ringing 1...2...3...), inform you when someone answers, and then give the person’s name (if provided)
For more information visit
TELUS Relay Service

Accessible Vehicles
Our bus fleet is 100% accessible, with ramps, a kneeling (lowering) front entrance,
priority seating
for seniors and others with reduced mobility, accessible stop request buttons and scooter/wheelchair access.
Features on the LRT system
include accessible doorways and ramps on train cars, a priority waiting area on train platforms, colour contrast and tactile edging and striping on stairs and platforms, automatic station door openers, elevators and escalators. Platforms and mezzanine levels of stations have audible and visual announcements.
Bus Types
Articulated Buses
With 20 feet more space than a regular 40-foot low floor bus, articulated buses help to reduce crowding on popular routes. These buses can fit up to 110 passengers and are also equipped with bike racks.
The inside of the bus looks much the same as the regular low floor buses. There are two positions for wheelchairs/scooters in the front seating area. The front of the bus lowers and has a flip-out ramp for easier access.

Community Buses
Community buses are used in areas where a larger, full-size bus cannot manoeuvre as easily as its mini counterpart. ETS also uses these buses in areas and times of low ridership and can bridge the gap between low floor buses and DATS door-to-door service on some routes.
Community buses have the same accessibility features as full-sized buses, with low floor entry, a fold-out ramp, and flip-up seats in the priority seating area. They operate on standard schedules, and riders pay regular ETS fares.
Many community bus routes are designed with senior's destinations in mind. These routes travel to areas with higher populations of seniors, stopping at destinations such as seniors' residential complexes, medical/professional buildings, social and recreational activity centres, retail complexes, and transit centres. However, these routes are available for use by any ETS rider.

Rider Respect
You can help create a more supportive transit experience for everyone by exercising respect and kindness when interacting with your fellow riders.
There are a few simple ways riders can help make everyone feel more welcome:
Put people first.
Show you care and offer your seat to riders who may need it more. Respect priority seating and shared spaces
Remember that not all disabilities are visible.
Disabilities can be visible, invisible or episodic. Show kindness and respect for your fellow riders.
Don't make assumptions. Ask before you help.
Recognize and respect everyone’s autonomy.  If you are in a situation where you think someone may need help, don’t make any assumptions and make sure to ask, “Would you like some help?” Never touch someone without their permission.
Arc Tap Exemption Program
Some riders are not able to tap an Arc card due to a physical or cognitive disability. In order to help these riders independently use conventional transit, ETS has introduced an Arc display pass, which eligible riders can use to access all public transit services in Edmonton.
The Arc display pass can only be used on ETS vehicles. It cannot be used on other regional transit systems.
For more information, contact ETS Customer Programs by calling 780-496-8321 or emailing
etscustomerprograms@edmonton.ca
.
How to apply
Contact ETS Customer Programs by calling
780-496-8321
or emailing
etscustomerprograms@edmonton.ca
.
You will receive an application form via email or mail.
Fill out the form (or a designate can fill it out for you).
Submit the completed form 1 of 3 ways:
Email:
etscustomerprograms@edmonton.ca
Mail to: Edmonton Transit Service Customer Programs, 15th Floor, 10111 104 Avenue NW, Edmonton, Alberta T5J 0J4
Drop off in person: Edmonton Service Centre, 2nd Floor, 10111 104 Avenue NW
ETS will contact you when your application has been processed. If your application is approved, ETS will provide you with your pass and instructions for fare payment.
Note that you will be asked to formally declare that your disability makes it impossible for you to tap your Arc card.
Verification from a healthcare professional may be requested to confirm eligibility.
