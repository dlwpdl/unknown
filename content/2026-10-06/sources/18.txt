Located in northeast Edmonton on the north bank of the North Saskatchewan River, Rundle Park is a popular destination that offers a wide range of outdoor activities and scenic river valley views.
Home to sports fields, multi-use trails, the
Rundle Park Golf Course
and picnic sites, to name a few, it is the ideal spot for families, fitness enthusiasts and nature-lovers to enjoy all that nature has to offer. The park space is also anchored by the
ACT Aquatic and Recreation Centre
, offering year-round indoor swimming and fitness amenities right on site.

Playground Access
The playground features paved access, paved pathway connections between play structures and seating areas that include picnic tables or benches located near each structure.
Some play structures offer both ground-level and ramped access options to provide options for inclusive play.
Rundle Family Centre
The Rundle Family Centre is divided into 2 distinct sections.
Access to the public park amenities is via the west-side entrance.
The
main doors
are equipped with door openers. This side of the building offers year-round access to accessible washrooms, seating, day-use lockers, an indoor skate change, a warming station and a water fountain with a bottle refill station.
The eastern half is reserved for
exclusive-use rentals
.
Parking
Accessible parking stalls are available in each parking lot adjacent to a facility
Bike racks are available onsite for secure lock-up
Trails and Pathways
Paved trails link the accessible parking areas directly to the primary trail network. Pondside viewpoints are available along the paved trail system. These trails also connect the parking lots to the ACT Aquatic and Recreation Centre, Rundle Family Centre and the IceWay entrance.
Winter Season
After each snowfall, staff will prioritize snow and ice removal for your safety and comfort. This includes clearing primary pathways, accessible parking areas, and key facility routes.
Learn more about
standard snow-clearing service
. Please use caution, especially near spots where the trail meets the ice surface and along ramps. If you experience access problems, please call 311 to report your concerns.
