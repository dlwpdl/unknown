# Nicknames and linked business identity

User request: users can change their nickname after onboarding; admins can change it too. From the admin user directory/detail, choose an existing business and link that user as its verified owner/operator. A linked user can write under the business identity (for example Jimoco) from their profile. Keep personal and company identities separate.

Assumptions: reuse the existing Expo 57 app/profile settings, AAL2 admin user detail, merchant account connection RPC/forms, and protected business draft publisher. One selected member at a time opens the existing reviewed connection flow; no mass ownership transfer or automatic business creation. Existing consent, confirmation/note, owner/operator restrictions and optimistic revisions remain. User authorization covers implementation; the existing app/server/store deployment hold remains.

Success criteria:
- Profile settings has an accessible nickname editor: trim, 2–20 Unicode characters, unique case-insensitively, explicit save/cancel, busy/error feedback. Self-change updates auth context immediately. Admin change refreshes detail and directory; both reject stale names and wrong accounts.
- Admin user detail enables business access and lets the admin choose a business and connect the selected member using the same guarded owner/operator form as the business page. Existing owners are never overwritten. Existing posts, membership, contacts and personal nickname stay intact.
- Profile clearly names connected businesses and opens the existing business writer. Publishing is rechecked on the server; revoked/unaccepted links cannot publish. No invented role/permission framework.
- Explicit business publications show company identity in feed/search/detail/saved/author/company lists and public reader, with company navigation. Keep the real personal actor ID for moderation, reporting and authorization. Merely linking/curating a personal post does not make it an official business post; do not infer historical authorship.
- New public identity reads expose only bounded public business metadata and preserve hidden/blocked/inactive/deleted/consent restrictions. Basic businesses can publish without an automatic paid-tier/status change; review eligibility remains separate.

Implementation slices: (1) shared nickname validation/RPC + self/admin UI; (2) user-side business selection reusing current connection forms and profile writer entry; (3) explicit business attribution + shared post hydration/readers, security and compatibility checks.
